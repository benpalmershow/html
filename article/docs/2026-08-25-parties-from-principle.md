> Parties from principle, especially abstract speculative principle, are known only to modern times, and are, perhaps, the most extraordinary and unaccountable phaenomenon, that has yet appeared in human affairs. - David Hume, [*Essays, Moral, Political, and Literary*](https://davidhume.org/texts/empl1/pg) (Essay VIII)

Hume distinguishes three sources of faction: interest, principle, and affection. Interest is rational and predictable. Affection is tribal but personal. Principle is different.

Modern ideological parties run on principle. They fight over abstractions—equality, liberty, identity—that no one can fully define but everyone is willing to die for. Unlike interest, which bends toward compromise, principle is absolute. Unlike affection, which is bounded by familiarity, principle scales to millions who will never meet.

The result is permanent hostility. Two people traveling opposite directions on a broad road pass each other easily. Two people reasoning from opposite principles cannot. They must convert or destroy.

This is our politics. Not a dispute over resources or power, but a war of worldviews.
