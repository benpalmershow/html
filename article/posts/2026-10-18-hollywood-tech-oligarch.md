
> NYT: Hollywood Thinks the Tech Oligarch Must Be Stopped 
> Doc Riter: Americans empowered by AI think Hollywood Must Be Stopped
Technology is not a villain because Sorkin made a film about Zuck. 
They are not liars, fabulists, and narcissists any more than Hollywood.

The New York Times suggests Hollywood has decided the tech oligarch must be stopped, framing Silicon Valley's power as the new existential threat worth dramatizing on screen. Doc Riter flips the lens: "Americans empowered by AI think Hollywood Must Be Stopped."

The core point is straightforward: technology itself isn't villainous just because a filmmaker made a movie about Zuckerberg. And tech founders aren't inherently liars, fabulists, or narcissists in any way that distinguishes them categorically from Hollywood.

That asymmetry is telling. Hollywood has spent decades mythmaking and shaping cultural narratives, often with little self-awareness about its own concentration of power and incentives. The industry that built its fortune on selling stories now frets about who controls the next storytelling machine. 

Instead of a morality play pitting good creators against evil technologists, the more honest take is a competition over attention, distribution, and cultural influence. Both industries chase those things. Calling one side uniquely corrupt while the other rides to the rescue doesn't hold up. The power struggle is real, but the saints and villains are evenly distributed.
