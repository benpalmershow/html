
> NYT: Latest Round of Rallies By ‘No Kings’ Group Focuses on Early Votes
> Doc Riter: White Liberal woman in Prospect Park holds racist sign “because Black votes matter too”

The New York Times covers the latest round of "No Kings" rallies focusing on getting out the early vote. Doc Riter points to a photo-op moment: a white liberal woman in Prospect Park holding a sign that reads "because Black votes matter too."

The contradiction is stark. If the premise is that Black votes matter, the "too" implies they don't already - or that the movement needs to be reminded of it. It's a classic case of performative allyship where the focus subtly shifts back to the person holding the sign.

The sentiment might be well-intentioned, but the phrasing betrays a worldview that treats the cause as something the holder is graciously extending to others, rather than something rooted in basic equality. It centers the white ally rather than the people the message is supposedly about.

This is the kind of symbolic gesture that dominates modern protest culture: more concerned with signaling one's own virtue than with precision or clarity. The message doesn't persuade skeptics; it reassures the sign-holder that they matter for holding it. And that's the problem - when activism becomes primarily about self-affirmation, the actual goal gets lost.
