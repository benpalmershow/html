### Means–ends analysis (MEA)
Tariffs are a means to challenging a China dominated market hegemony. The end goal is a reaffirmed American hegemony; integrated international harmony with American partners and American production. However, an inconsistent and hawkish foreign policy, anti-austerity pet projects on both sides, and unaligned bicameral politics as ostensibly esteemed checks and balances have resulted in fatter checks for higher balances. Debt grows because democracies don’t vote for higher taxes and less spending. 

Another New York Times piece champions the "technocrat" solution: let credentialed experts cut through politics and deliver abundance, especially on housing and regulation. Doc Riter isn't buying the brand.

The skepticism starts with the proposed fixes. Pointing to Europe's lower pilot hour requirements or demanding the federal government override local zoning to punish recalcitrant jurisdictions feels less like sound policy than abstract engineering. "No thanks" to trading local control for a top-down diktat.

The inflation diagnosis also misses the blame game. The cost of living spike came under Biden, not Trump's tariffs, but both approaches aim at the same stated goal: lower prices. One tries to do it by spending less and producing more at home; the other leaned into fiscal expansion. 

Doc Riter's larger point is about means and ends. Tariffs aren't about prices in isolation - they're a means to challenge China's market dominance with the end goal of reasserting American leverage while integrating with trusted partners. But that strategic goal collides with domestic political reality: no democracy votes for steep tax hikes and spending cuts. Pet projects persist across parties, foreign policy swings, and checks and balances make hard tradeoffs nearly impossible. So debt keeps growing.

The real question isn't whether experts have good ideas. It's whether a technocratic approach can convince "needy humans" to accept the costs of those ideas. Without that political coalition, "unleashing the technocrats" is just another form of wishful thinking.
