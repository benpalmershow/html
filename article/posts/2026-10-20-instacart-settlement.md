### ⚖️ DOJ and EEOC Secure $5M Settlement with Instacart Over Discrimination Against U.S. Workers

DOJ and EEOC announced a $5,000,000 joint settlement with Instacart over allegations of discrimination against U.S. and American workers based on citizenship status and national origin in violation of the Immigration and Nationality Act and Title VII.

The agencies found that Instacart's hiring and recruitment practices for positions advertised as part of the Permanent Labor Certification (PERM) process intentionally discriminated against U.S. workers by preferring workers with temporary employment visas. Specifically, Instacart required U.S. workers to submit paper applications by mail for PERM positions while allowing electronic applications through its standard process, creating additional burdens that prevented many from being timely considered.

Under the settlement terms:
- Instacart will establish a $4,000,000 settlement fund to compensate affected applicants and deterred applicants
- $1,000,000 in civil penalties to the United States
- Required to post PERM positions on its public career website and accept electronic applications
- Must provide anti-discrimination training, revise policies, and submit to monitoring

This is the fifteenth settlement under the Justice Department's Protecting U.S. Workers Initiative since its relaunch in 2025. Since the initiative's original launch, the Department has secured over $34 million in back pay for U.S. workers and over $16 million in civil penalties.
