Housing is still sliding, the labor market is coasting sideways, and the market has fully priced a Fed hold plus a Democratic midterm year.

## Housing: two more soft prints

| Indicator | Agency | Period | Value | Prior | MoM | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pending Home Sales Index | NAR | Sep 2026 | 66.0 | 71.2 | -7.3% | -11.3% |
| Housing Market Index | NAHB | Oct 2026 | 29 | 32 | -9.4% | -21.6% |

The Pending Home Sales Index is the more important of the pair, because contracts signed this month become closed sales in one or two months. September came in at 66.0, down 7.3% from August and 11.3% from a year earlier. That is the lowest print of 2026, and it sits 13.8% below the May peak of 76.6. The index has now fallen for four straight months.

Builders are not cheerful either. The NAHB Housing Market Index dropped to 29 from 32, which is 21.6% below October 2025 and the weakest reading in the series. The index has to clear 50 before more builders than not call conditions good, so builders are describing conditions as bad by a wide margin. Rising rates, elevated prices, and flat wages explain the squeeze on both sides of the transaction.

## Labor: steady, with a small pullback

| Indicator | Agency | Period | Value | Prior | MoM | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Employment Trends Index | Conference Board | Sep 2026 | 107.56 | 108.08 | -0.5% | +1.2% |

The Conference Board Employment Trends Index landed at 107.56 in September, down 0.5% from a revised August reading of 108.08, which is the high of 2026. That August revision matters: the index spent the summer climbing, from 107.01 in May to 108.08 in August, and September gave a little of it back. The level is still 1.2% above a year earlier. A composite that stalls in the high 107s is a labor market holding its shape rather than one gaining ground. The blend of job openings, industrial production, and business confidence moves ahead of the unemployment rate and payrolls.

## Prediction markets: six new prints

| Market | Latest odds | Recent read | Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| FOMC rate decision, Oct 27-28 | Hold 86¢, hike 12¢, cut 0¢ | Sep 29: hold 30¢ | Hold +56 points |
| US recession in 2026 | 6¢ (Kalshi 5¢) | Oct 4: 8¢ | -26 points since Apr 17 (32¢) |
| Senate control | Dem 64¢ / Rep 36¢ | Aug 25: 50/50 | Dem +14 points |
| House control | Dem 92¢ / Rep 8¢ | May 12: 49/51 | Dem +43 points |
| Hormuz traffic normal by Dec 31 | 12¢ | Oct 19: 14¢ | -22 points since Aug 24 (34¢) |
| Iranian regime falls by end of 2026 | 4¢ | Oct 19: 4¢ | -29 points since Apr 17 (33¢) |

The Fed repricing is the biggest single move on the board. Markets spent September pricing a second consecutive hike, with hold odds bottoming at 30¢ on September 29 while hike odds hit 70¢. Three weeks later the trade flipped completely: hold at 86¢, a 25 basis point hike at 12¢, and a cut at zero. The statement lands at 2:00 PM ET on October 28.

Recession odds at 6% line up with the same resilience: real GDP grew at a 3.1% annualized pace in the third quarter, unemployment held near 4.2%, and the Sahm rule sits well below its 0.50 trigger. Nobody is forecasting a contraction, and the market is not pricing one.

The midterm markets are lopsided. Democrats at 64¢ in the Senate need a net gain of four seats, and at 92¢ in the House they need roughly three. Generic ballot advantage and mid-30s approval ratings for the president are doing the work. The House number has drifted from 94¢ on October 4 down to 90¢ on October 19 and back to 92¢, which is noise in a market this lopsided.

The two geopolitical markets both hardened. The chance that Strait of Hormuz traffic returns to normal by December 31 sits at 12¢ on $14.0 million of volume, down from 34¢ in late August. Resolution needs the IMF PortWatch seven-day average at 60 transits per day, while AIS-visible traffic runs at roughly 3 to 7 per day, or 3% to 7% of the 100 to 138 per day pre-crisis baseline. Iran's October 4 demands for reopening have not moved the number. Regime change by year end sits at 4¢ on $26.7 million of volume, down from 33¢ in mid-April.

## The pattern

Housing is the weak link in this batch. Contract activity down 11.3% year over year, builder sentiment at 29, and prices that have not adjusted enough to clear the market. The labor composite gave back 0.5% in September, and the Fed is priced to sit still through the meeting while that weakness plays out. If the housing data keeps sliding, the 6% recession price becomes the interesting one to watch.
