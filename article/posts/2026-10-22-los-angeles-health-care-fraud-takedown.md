### ⚖️ Los Angeles Health Care Fraud Takedown: 15 Defendants, Nearly $2B in Loss

The Justice Department announced charges against 15 defendants, including six medical professionals, for health care fraud schemes that collectively resulted in over $1.8 billion in fraudulent claims to government and private health care programs. Eight defendants were arrested today.

The charges include the largest substance abuse treatment fraud scheme ever prosecuted by the Department, plus a $134 million scheme involving unnecessary atherectomy procedures that injured patients, and three hospice fraud schemes totaling over $138 million.

**Substance abuse treatment fraud (over $1.6 billion):** Nathan Young, David Young, Honey Phipps, and Cody Phipps were charged with a years-long scheme to traffic vulnerable patients from around the country to Southern California for sham substance abuse treatment billed to private insurance. The Youngs controlled dozens of purported treatment companies and directed body brokers to target vulnerable populations, including individuals who were unhoused and in poverty. Patients were lured with free flights, money, apartments, cars, and drugs, then recycled through facilities while billing insurance regardless of whether treatment was actually provided. Nicholas Moore pleaded guilty for his role as an area manager, admitting to assisting in the submission of $199 million in fraudulent claims.

**Invasive surgical procedures ($135 million):** Dr. Harold Tabaie, a cardiothoracic surgeon, and Hany Malek, a chiropractor posing as a medical doctor, were charged with billing Medicare for medically unnecessary atherectomies at two Los Angeles clinics. The case was identified through data analytics showing Tabaie was an extreme outlier for atherectomy procedures in the United States from 2016 through 2023.

**Hospice fraud (over $138 million across three schemes):** Geoffrey Jimenez was charged with operating a fraudulent hospice scheme through Transitions Hospice, Inc., enrolling non-terminal Medicare beneficiaries and billing for medically unnecessary services. Dr. Richard Keech was charged with falsely certifying beneficiaries as terminally ill to receive hospice care. Armani Raffi was charged with orchestrating a multi-entity hospice fraud scheme through four interconnected providers, resulting in approximately $21 million in false claims.

**Additional charges:** Two California doctors were charged with accepting illegal kickbacks for authorizing millions of dollars in medically unnecessary genetic testing orders. Thomas Steven Mladinich was charged with receiving illegal kickbacks for patient referrals to a clinical treatment facility. Christina Mareik was charged for her role in a nearly $270 million scheme to defraud Medi-Cal through fraudulent pharmacy claims. Dejun Wang was charged with the prohibited sale of prescription drug samples.

The cases were brought under President Trump's Task Force to Eliminate Fraud, a whole-of-government effort chaired by Vice President J.D. Vance.
