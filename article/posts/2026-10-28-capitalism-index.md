The [Global Capitalism Index (GCI)](https://capitalismindex.com/) from the Democracy and Capitalism Lab at the University of Virginia ranks countries based on institutions that support market systems. 

**United States (2025):**
- GCI Composite: 95.00 (rank #2 globally)

**Subindex scores (2025):**
| Subindex | Score | Description |
| - | - | - |
| Market Supporting Policy (PLCY) | 92.02 | Government policy, regulatory quality, infrastructure enabling markets |
| Market Competition (COMP) | 93.64 | Competitive intensity, creative destruction, regulatory environment |
| Capital Market Sophistication (SOPH) | 78.93 | Depth, breadth, resilience of capital markets |
| Banking System (BANK) | 81.96 | Solvency, access, regulatory quality of banking system |
| New Business Formation (BUSI) | 84.33 | Ease of starting, operating, growing new firms |
| Free Flow of Goods and Capital (FLOW) | 84.73 | Openness to cross-border goods, capital, people |
| Property Rights (PROP) | 86.42 | Private property, contract enforcement, rule of law, corruption |
| Labor Market Openness (LABR) | 76.88 | Flexibility, accessibility, inclusivity of labor market |

**Property Rights:** At 86.42, the U.S. Property Rights subindex remains relatively strong, but it trails its overall competitiveness. The GCI measures the strength of private property rights, contract enforcement, rule of law, and freedom from corruption - core institutional foundations that underpin market exchange. Weakening in any of these areas erodes predictability for investment and long-term planning.

Despite yesterday's NPR Marketplace segment framing property rights concerns as the major story, the data shows the U.S. remains the world's second most capitalist economy overall (95.00), behind only Switzerland (96.9). The ranking reflects the breadth of market-supporting institutions, not a single issue in isolation.

Source: [Global Capitalism Index](https://capitalismindex.com/profile/united-states)
