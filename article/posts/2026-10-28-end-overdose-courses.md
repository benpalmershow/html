Ketamine, commonly known as "Special K", has become increasingly prevalent as a recreational party drug on college campuses. Originally developed as a medical and veterinary anesthetic, illicit ketamine is often snorted or ingested at parties and raves for its dissociative effects.

**Key Risks:**
- **Dissociation** - Users can enter a "k-hole", becoming temporarily unable to move, speak, or call for help.
- **Impaired coordination** - High risk of falls, injuries, or accidents, especially in social settings.
- **Polydrug dangers** - Mixing ketamine with alcohol, benzodiazepines, or opioids significantly increases the risk of respiratory depression.
- **Vulnerability** - Those incapacitated from ketamine are at higher risk of injury, assault, or being unable to seek assistance.

[End Overdose](https://endoverdose.net/courses/) provides training that addresses emerging drug trends like ketamine, focusing on harm reduction, recognizing when someone needs help, and knowing when to call for emergency assistance. As use continues to spread on campuses, awareness of these risks is critical.
