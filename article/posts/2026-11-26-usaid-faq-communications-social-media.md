# FAQ on Communications with Social Media Companies

On October 5, 2023, State issued additional guidance (23 STATE 108554) regarding communications with social media companies in light of the Fifth Circuit's decision in Missouri v. Biden. As a result of the decision, which significantly narrowed the injunction previously issued in the case, USAID was no longer prohibited from communicating with social media companies about content moderation in concert with State (or other parties). GC drafted this FAQ document in October 2023 as an additional resource to help USAID staff address common questions in light of the Missouri v. Biden litigation. The case, renamed Murthy v. Missouri, was subsequently appealed to the Supreme Court and ultimately dismissed on procedural grounds.

As a result of the Supreme Court's dismissal of the case, USAID now has a greater ability to communicate with social media companies. In September 2024, light edits were made to this guidance in light of the developments in the Murthy v. Missouri litigation and updated guidance from the Administration.

Bureaus and Missions should continue to follow this GC FAQ document, which is consistent with State and White House guidance. White House guidance encourages departments and agencies to engage appropriately with technology companies in order to address grave threats and not to be dissuaded by litigation like Murthy v. Missouri, from engaging in this important work. It underscores the importance of avoiding action that could be perceived as coercion. Similarly, State's guidance notes that State Department activities involving or pertaining to social media companies should continue, but highlights that the United States Government (USG) cannot significantly encourage or coerce social media companies to take content moderation actions in violation of the First Amendment.

This GC FAQ continues to apply until otherwise revised or replaced. Bureaus and Missions should continue to work closely with GC/RLO and contact Chioma Ayogu (cayogu@usaid.gov), Megan Metcalf (mmetcalf@usaid.gov) and Jahn Olson (jolson@usaid.gov) with any questions about this FAQ, specific scenarios, or upcoming engagements.

## Q&A

**Q #1: Does State's guidance or the recent litigation in Murthy v. Missouri prohibit or limit USAID's ability to use social media to promote its work?**

A. No. USAID can continue to use official USAID social media accounts to publicize the work of USAID, in compliance with ADS 558 (Use of Social Media for Public Engagement).

**Q #2: Is USAID prohibited from communicating with social media companies or working on issues involving information integrity and resilience?**

A. No. USAID can continue to work with social media companies in accordance with the guidance provided here and in close coordination with GC/RLO. The guidance is designed to help USAID continue its work to advance human rights and protect freedom of expression online, consistent with legal mandates in the Foreign Assistance Act of 1961 (FAA) and annual appropriations acts. This may include work to support information integrity, digital democracy, election security, digital security and media. For example, funds were specifically made available under Section 7032(g) of the FY 2023 Appropriations Act (Division K of P.L. 117-328) to advance digital security and counter disinformation in USAID partner countries.

**Q #3: Is USAID prohibited from partnering with social media companies on activities that do not involve content moderation?**

A. No. USAID may continue to engage in these partnerships. Examples: digital literacy bootcamps for university students in partner countries; ad buys on social media outlets to publicize USAID activities or programs in partner countries; development of online tools that civil society can use to help address development challenges or business sustainability.

**Q #4: What are some considerations to keep in mind when communicating with social media companies in general or with respect to content moderation in particular?**

A. USAID employees and contractors should only request content be removed, retained, posted, or promoted consistent with the social media company's policies. Avoid actions that could be considered "coercion" or "significant encouragement." Clearly communicate that any action taken in response to a request from USAID is voluntary. USAID employees and contractors must not be involved in or attempt to influence the content moderation decisions of social media companies. USAID employees and contractors should only make content moderation requests related to USAID staff, activities, or objectives. Exceptions must be cleared with GC/RLO.

**Q #5: What are some examples of actions that could be considered "coercion?"**

A. Depending on context: public calls for action from the USG pressuring social media companies to endorse or adopt industry or international principles or standards; public statements urging social media companies to take specific action regarding content moderation or publicly criticizing them for failing to do so; public statements noting that social media should be held accountable for failing to take action through fundamental reforms, such as regulatory action.

**Q #6: What are some examples of actions that could be considered "significant encouragement?"**

A. Depending on circumstances: actions inside and outside meetings with social media companies that identify certain posts as misinformation, identify options for labeling or addressing the misinformation and asking for amplification of approved content the USG has determined are accurate; enlisting the assistance of senior leadership at USAID to facilitate resolution of a content moderation request; repeated requests for social media companies to change their content moderation policies, particularly where the requests do not end until the social media company makes changes that satisfy the USG's concerns.

## Content Moderation Requests

**Q #7: Can USAID staff or contractors request removal of content that violates the Social Media Company's terms of service/use or policies?**

A. Yes. Content moderation requests must be related to USAID staff, activities or objectives. USAID employees and contractors may only contact social media companies to request removal of content that violates the social media company's terms of service/use. Exceptions must be cleared with GC/RLO. Such content frequently includes: violent or graphic content, terrorist propaganda or support, harassment, spam, intellectual property infringement, impersonation of USG officials or official USG accounts, or certain privacy violations.

**Q #8: How does USAID request removal of violent, harassing or other content that violates the social media company's terms of service/use?**

A. Outreach to request removal must follow the procedure outlined: (1) Content of Request - cleared by GC/RLO, must identify the content, include a screenshot or direct link, identify the terms of service or terms of use violated, must not be coercive in tone or imply adverse consequences; (2) Means of Transmission - using existing channels as identified in platform terms of service, terms of use, or policies; (3) Escalation - submitted and shepherded by an appropriate POC; senior leadership in USAID/W or Missions must not be engaged to help facilitate resolution unless requested by social media companies; (4) Follow Up Outreach - appropriate follow-up after a reasonable period of time; appeals or requests for reconsideration must follow the social media company's policy.

**Q #9: How does USAID request Removal of content that Poses a Threat to the Safety of USAID Staff or Recipients of USAID Foreign Assistance?**

A. USAID may request that social media companies remove content that creates a serious threat to the safety of USAID staff or facilities, or to recipients of USAID funds. Mission staff should contact the RSO or the DS Office of Protective Intelligence Investigations (DS/TIA/PII) at DSPIIDutyAgent@state.gov and copy the EXO for the Mission. USAID/Washington staff should contact the USAID Command Center at USAIDCommandCenter@usaid.gov. The email should include a screenshot or direct link to the content, an explanation of the threat posed, and information regarding the account owner/manager, if known. Any PII should be password protected.

**Q #10: How does USAID request Retention of content?**

A. All requests for retention of content must demonstrate that the content did not violate the social media company's terms of service or terms of use. USAID staff must follow the same procedures regarding means of transmission, escalation, and follow-up outreach as for requests for removal.

**Q #11: How does USAID request Promotion of content?**

A. USAID and its partners should rely on existing channels and tools developed by the social media company (e.g., paid boosting) to promote or raise awareness of USAID funded or supported activities. USAID may develop partnerships or execute memorandums of understanding (MOUs) or other non-binding agreements with social media companies to amplify or promote USAID projects or activities. USAID should ensure the "ask" or purpose of the partnership is clearly communicated. USAID should not conduct outreach to social media companies on behalf of its partners to encourage social media companies or online platforms to promote or post content unless it is part of a partnership, MOU, or other non-binding agreement.

**Q #12: Can multiple OUs or Missions send content moderation requests regarding the same content or enlist the assistance of senior leadership to help underscore the importance of the request or raise its profile?**

A. No. OUs and Missions must follow the process outlined in this guidance. Senior leadership cannot be enlisted to help raise the profile or facilitate resolution of a content moderation request. OUs and Missions should coordinate where possible to ensure that the social media company is not receiving an influx of requests regarding identical content from multiple parts of USAID.

**Q #13: Can USAID staff or contractors contact social media companies to make content moderation requests on behalf of recipients of foreign assistance or members of the public?**

A. USAID employees and contractors should only make content moderation requests related to USAID staff, activities, or objectives. Members of the public should be encouraged to contact the social media companies directly, rather than referring these incidents to USAID for follow up or action. Exceptions must be cleared with GC/RLO.

**Q #14: Can USAID form partnerships with social media companies?**

A. Yes. USAID may develop informal or formal partnerships or execute a memorandum of understanding (MOU) or other non-binding agreement with social media companies to amplify or promote USAID projects or activities or partner to address a development challenge or otherwise meet development objectives.

**Q #15: Can USAID staff or contractors convene events that involve discussion of 1) common approaches taken by social media companies to safeguarding users, promoting human rights online, information integrity, or content moderation as well as 2) opportunities to strengthen these approaches by adopting or aligning with international standards, principles or best practices?**

A. Yes. USAID staff and contractors must coordinate closely with GC/RLO when convening multi-stakeholder dialogues, listening sessions, or other public discussions. These discussions should not focus on any one social media company's policies nor should they explicitly or implicitly encourage participants to advocate for changes in policy to social media companies and should not significantly encourage social media companies to take action regarding specific content. Additional caution should be exercised if these dialogues or discussions include participants from social media companies.

**Q #16: Can USAID staff or contractors have one-on-one discussions with social media companies about their policies for supporting users or promoting human rights online?**

A. Yes, but discussions with social media companies on the subject of their policies, practices or approaches to safeguarding users or promoting human rights online or opportunities to strengthen their policies by adopting or aligning with international standards, principles or best practices may only occur in strict accordance with this guidance and only in cases where it advances clear foreign policy objectives under the FAA or other applicable law. USAID staff and contractors must make clear that participation is entirely voluntary, that the conversations are intended to be informational only, that social media companies are under no obligation to provide information or create new data sets or documents for USAID, and that social media companies should not provide personally identifiable information (PII) or non-public information to USAID.

**Q #17: Can USAID reach out to social media companies to resolve "customer service issues" related to its posts?**

A. Yes, USAID can reach out to a social media company to resolve "customer service issues" related to its posts using the social media company's existing channels or tools. If there is no existing process or point of contact to resolve these issues for users, USAID can develop partnerships with social media companies to help better amplify or promote USAID projects or activities. USAID can also flag this as a general issue for users, but cannot imply that the social media company is expected to develop a process for use by the USG.

**Q #18: How do the guidance from State and USAID address or take into consideration freedom of expression?**

A. This guidance reflects USAID's longstanding commitment to protecting and promoting freedom of expression, and free and independent media consistent with the United States' responsibilities as a party to the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which protects freedom of expression, and the right to freedom of speech protected by the First Amendment to the United States Constitution.

**Q #19: Where can I find additional guidance?**

A. Please contact your GC or RLO and contact Chioma Ayogu (cayogu@usaid.gov), Meghan Metcalf (mmetcalf@usaid.gov) and Jahn Olson (jolson@usaid.gov) for additional guidance regarding specific scenarios or upcoming engagements.
